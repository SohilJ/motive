# Questions that change the animation

Ask about meaning until the roles and consequential change are sufficiently clear. Use the answers already available. A short conversation may suffice; a vague concept may need several exchanges. Do not collect preferences merely to appear collaborative.

## Example: centralizing data

First ambiguity: gathering information, making sources cooperate, or revealing relationships?

- Gathering → should the sources retain their identities, or become a unified whole?
- Cooperating → is the benefit synchronization, or translating differences into something shared?
- Revealing → should connections emerge between the sources, or should a larger pattern appear across the whole?

The development conversation selected a unified whole that creates something new. That led to three distinct mechanisms: unequal capsules reorganize into a common rhythm; a shared stem opens new branches; overlapping parts reveal an opening that belongs to their arrangement. The question changed the geometry and choreography.

## Rejected trial: resilience, with all answers delegated

The user delegated the remaining semantic choices. The authored interpretation was adaptation: a connected span shifts support toward a load that alternates between left and right. Pressure preceded reconfiguration, and the span steadied under the same load. This gave the loop a causal structure, but the user rejected its visual language: attention and visual weight traveled from side to side instead of remaining centered and symmetrical.

The delegation workflow was valid; the composition was not. Technical checks and a coherent story did not establish a stylistic fit. Before proposing a mechanism, identify its fixed central anchor and how mirrored or rotationally coordinated changes preserve the balance. Discard mechanisms that require a migrating focal point unless the user explicitly changes the composition requirement.

One possible replacement would apply opposing pressure to a centered structure, with supports redistributing symmetrically around its core. This is an unbuilt proposal, not an approved interpretation. If a future resilience brief means personal recovery, ecological diversity, or network reliability, discover that meaning first and then develop a centered mechanism appropriate to it.

## Recognize when to act

- “I don't know” → make a recommendation with contrasting, concrete consequences.
- “Show me all” → small concept studies can reveal preferences sooner than another interview.
- “You choose the answers” → choose and record the missing semantics, then build the chosen mechanism.
- A correction about color or outline → fix that detail without reopening the meaning.
- A correction about centering or symmetry → revise the staging and carry the requirement into future concept selection; a finish change alone will not fix it.
- “This is boring” → return to the relationship and transformation. More particles or stronger easing may leave the idea just as weak.
- An existing precise metaphor → do not require an interview that cannot change it.

Separate authored choices, observations from sources, technical checks, and user approval. Preserve successful rules without turning one metaphor into the only permitted way to express a concept.
